# Technical Evaluation and Implementation Report: Replacing Timestepped Real-Valued MNA with Dynamic-Phasor / Shifted-Frequency Simulation in an Educational AC Electricity Mod for Vintage Story

## TL;DR

- **A dynamic-phasor (complex-envelope / shifted-frequency) solver running at the game tick rate (~30 Hz) can replace the 1 kHz real-valued MNA solver for steady and slowly-varying operation, cutting per-network solves by roughly 30× while producing *better*-looking scope traces, because the oscilloscope synthesizes the carrier analytically (`Re{X(t)·e^{jωt}}`) at any resolution rather than from ~20 samples/cycle.** This is the recommended target architecture, but only as the quasi-steady half of a **hybrid** solver.
- **Fast discrete events (switch closure mid-cycle, rectifier commutation, faults) violate the dynamic-phasor bandwidth assumption and must still be handled by a short burst of real-valued transient MNA**, spliced in on event detection and handed back to phasor mode once successive cycles converge. This is exactly the "frequency-adaptive" (FAST) and SFA–EMT hybrid approach in the literature.
- **Do the cheap wins first.** Cache/reuse the LU factorization while topology and timestep are fixed (only re-do forward/back-substitution per tick), keep Electrical Age–style subsystem partitioning so only changed subsystems re-solve, and run only the subsystem(s) that actually have an oscilloscope attached at high fidelity. These deliver large speedups for a fraction of the engineering risk of a full complex-valued rewrite, and are prerequisites for the phasor work anyway.

## Key Findings

1. **The existing design maps cleanly onto the published Electrical Age (ELN) architecture.** ELN splits the world circuit into independent connected-component **`SubSystem`s** (the ELN wiki describes each as "a single instance of a complete MNA matrix"),\[1\] tied together by a **`RootSystem`** that couples interdependent subsystems through `InterSystem` "process" solves. Components inherit `Component → Bipole → {Resistor, Capacitor, Inductor, VoltageSource}`; nodes are `State` objects;\[1\] solver constants live in `MnaConst`. The solver is real-valued/time-domain and (from the class structure) linear-per-substep with no Newton–Raphson loop; nonlinearity is approximated by re-stamping component parameters between substeps via `process` classes. ELN oversamples the electrical solve relative to the game tick (config keys `electricalFrequancy` and `electricalInterSystemOverSampling`, e.g. an oversampling factor of 50). This is architecturally identical to the mod's 1 kHz real-valued MNA, so the migration analysis below transfers directly.

2. **Dynamic phasors are time-varying Fourier coefficients.** Following Sanders, Noworolski, Liu & Verghese's generalized averaging method, a near-periodic waveform `x(τ)` on a sliding window `τ∈(t−T, t]` is written `x(τ) = Σ_k X_k(t)·e^{jkω_sτ}`, with the k-th dynamic phasor `X_k(t) = (1/T)∫_{t−T}^{t} x(τ)·e^{−jkω_sτ} dτ`. Only a small set of dominant coefficients is retained. When the phasors vary slowly, they can be integrated with a **much larger timestep** than the underlying carrier — this is the entire source of the speedup.

3. **The speedup is large and quantified in the literature.** P. Zhang's UBC thesis on Shifted Frequency Analysis reports (verbatim): *"Speedups of more than fifty times over the traditional EMTP synchronous machine model were obtained for a case of mechanical torque step changes,"* and elsewhere in the body notes that *"the SFA model is in excess of seventy times faster than the phase-coordinate EMTP model when simulating the slow dynamics."* A more conservative, general figure comes from "Improving EMT simulations using frequency-shifted rational approximations" (*Electric Power Systems Research*), which found that *"these frequency shifts enabled a time-step size increase by a factor of 2.33 to 5.5 for the same target accuracy, thereby reducing computational effort."* For this mod, the dominant, directly-attributable factor is simply that the network solve moves from ~1000 Hz to ~30 Hz — about a 30× reduction in solves per second — while waveform *display* quality improves.

4. **Waveform quality improves, not degrades.** At 1 kHz, a 50 Hz carrier is sampled at only 20 points/cycle (a 60 Hz carrier ≈ 16.7 points/cycle) — visibly polygonal on a scope. In phasor mode the scope layer evaluates `v(t) = Re{Σ_k X_k·e^{jkωt}}` at whatever pixel resolution the display wants, so a single 30 Hz phasor update yields a perfectly smooth sinusoid, plus any retained harmonics, at arbitrary sample density.

5. **Multi-harmonic dynamic phasors capture ripple and machine harmonics.** Rectifier ripple, six-pulse DC ripple, and machine space harmonics are represented by carrying additional `X_k` for the relevant harmonic orders (e.g. k = 1 for fundamental, plus k = 2/6 for a diode-bridge DC side, or characteristic harmonics for a six-pulse converter). This is the multifrequency averaging of Caliskan, Verghese & Stankovic and the diode-bridge dynamic-phasor models of Yang et al.

## Details

### 1. Benefits — CPU savings and rendering quality

**Why it is cheaper.** In timestepped real-valued MNA the timestep is bounded by the need to *resolve the carrier*: to draw a clean 50/60 Hz waveform with ripple you need many samples per cycle, hence ~1 kHz. Dynamic-phasor / SFA removes the carrier from the state variables — the states become the slowly-varying complex envelopes `X_k(t)`. The stiffness that forced the small step is gone; the step is now bounded only by the *envelope* bandwidth (wind-driven amplitude drift, load changes), which is well under a few Hz. The network can therefore be solved once per game tick (~30 Hz) instead of ~1000 times per second.

**Magnitude of the win.** Purely from the rate change, per-network matrix solves drop by ~33× (1000/30). Because the mod's wind amplitude "drifts every tick" as a *slowly-varying envelope*, this is precisely the regime dynamic phasors are built for. Published SFA results support that this rate reduction preserves accuracy for envelope dynamics (Zhang/UBC: >50× for machine transients; Strunz's FAST: envelope following at the shift frequency).

**Cost offsets.** Each phasor solve is more expensive per solve than a real solve of the same node count: the unknowns are complex, so the MNA matrix is complex (≈ 2× the storage and ~3–4× the arithmetic of the equivalent real system if implemented as a 2N×2N real block system). Multi-harmonic phasors multiply the system size by the number of retained harmonics. Net: with a single dominant harmonic, ~30× fewer solves × ~3–4× per-solve cost ⇒ order-of-magnitude net CPU savings; with, say, 3 harmonics retained on a rectifier subsystem the win narrows but is still favourable because only that subsystem pays the multi-harmonic cost.

**Rendering.** The scope becomes a *synthesis* layer: given `X_k` at tick n and n+1, interpolate the envelope (linear or cubic) across the tick and evaluate `Re{Σ_k X_k(t)·e^{jkωt}}` at display resolution. This yields smoother traces than 1 kHz sampling and lets the scope zoom in time without re-running the solver.

**Validity condition.** SFA/dynamic phasors are valid when the signal is band-limited and the phasor (envelope) bandwidth is *strictly below the carrier frequency* (50/60 Hz). As stated in the multi-generator variable-frequency dynamic-phasor work, "it is essential to limit the bandwidth of the phasors to strictly below the carrier frequency."\[2\] Events with spectral content spilling near or above the carrier (sharp switching edges, commutation notches, faults) break the single-phasor approximation and need either many harmonics or a transient solve.

### 3. Proposed architecture — a hybrid FAST-style solver

**(a) Dynamic-phasor quasi-steady mode (default, ~30 Hz).** Each subsystem is stamped as a *complex* MNA system in the shifted-frequency domain. R stamps as `G`; an inductor stamps as complex admittance whose value depends on the carrier plus the SFA companion term; a capacitor likewise. Sources become complex phasor injections. Wind-driven amplitude changes are simply **per-tick updates to the source-phasor magnitude** — no re-solve of anything structural, just a new right-hand side (and, if only the RHS changes, only a back-substitution; see §5). Between ticks the scope interpolates the envelope.

**(b) Event-triggered transient splice mode (adaptive ~1 kHz).** A discrete event (switch toggle, breaker/fuse, rectifier device state change, fault) triggers the affected subsystem(s) to drop to `shift frequency = 0` — i.e. ordinary real-valued transient MNA at 1 kHz (or adaptive). The transient sim is **initialized from the phasor state**: instantaneous node voltages and inductor currents at the splice instant are reconstructed as `x(t₀)=Re{Σ_k X_k·e^{jkωt₀}}`, guaranteeing waveform continuity (no visible jump). The burst runs until steady state is re-detected.

**(c) Handoff back to phasor mode.** When the transient waveform has settled, extract phasors from it by demodulation / one-cycle DFT: `X_k = (1/T)∫ x(τ)e^{−jkω_sτ}dτ` over the last carrier cycle (a running/recursive sliding-window DFT is cheapest). Load those `X_k` as the new phasor state and resume 30 Hz stepping. The Tarazona–Marti SFA–EMT protocol and Strunz's FAST show this two-way transform is exact for band-limited signals.

**Steady-state detection.** Compare successive one-cycle phasor estimates from the transient waveform; when `‖X_k(cycle m) − X_k(cycle m−1)‖` falls below a threshold for a few consecutive cycles, declare steady and hand back. Alternatively track the derivative of the extracted envelope.

**Per-harmonic phasor sets for nonlinear loads.** Rectifier/motor subsystems carry a configured harmonic set `U = {k}`. The complex MNA is block-structured per harmonic; coupling between harmonics (from the switching/modulation functions) appears as off-diagonal blocks, following Caliskan–Verghese–Stankovic multifrequency averaging and the Yang et al. diode-bridge dynamic-phasor model (which conveniently includes 2nd and 6th harmonics on the DC link).

### 4. Caveats and risks

- **Where dynamic phasors break down:** fast switching with edges near/above the carrier, frequency excursions beyond the phasor bandwidth, and strongly nonlinear elements that need many harmonics for fidelity. Each retained harmonic adds a full complex block, eroding the speed advantage; there is a crossover point (roughly, when the harmonic count × complex-cost approaches the 30× rate advantage) beyond which a transient solve is cheaper. Detect this and prefer transient mode for such subsystems.
- **Complex-valued MNA complexity:** the matrix and all stamps become complex; storage ~2× and arithmetic ~3–4× per solve versus the real system. Implement either with a native complex type or the equivalent real 2N×2N block `[[Re, −Im],[Im, Re]]` formulation (see §6).
- **Time-varying frequency (generator speed changes the carrier).** Wind turbines change speed, so the carrier ω is not fixed. Two established options: (i) **instantaneous frequency tracking** — update the shift frequency ω(t) per tick (FAST allows the shift frequency to be adapted during simulation), which keeps the envelope slow; or (ii) a **common (single) reference frame** at nominal frequency, letting each machine's phasor rotate slowly at its slip from nominal. Demiray and the multi-generator variable-frequency dynamic-phasor work address exactly this.
- **Multiple asynchronous generators → beating.** If two generators run at slightly different frequencies, their phasors rotate at different rates in any common frame; the beat (difference frequency) appears as a slow envelope rotation, which the phasor solver represents correctly *provided the beat frequency stays below the phasor bandwidth*. Large frequency splits push the envelope bandwidth up and can force transient mode. This is a genuine correctness risk worth explicit testing.
- **DC subnetworks and rectifier coupling.** A rectifier couples an AC side (carrier ω) to a DC side (carrier 0). In dynamic-phasor terms the DC side is the k=0 phasor and the AC side the k=1 (and harmonics); the rectifier's switching function couples them. Getting this coupling right is the hardest modeling task; the Yang et al. and HVDC dynamic-phasor references are the templates. A pragmatic fallback: keep rectifier + DC load subsystems in transient mode and only phasor-simulate the pure-AC generation/transmission subsystems.
- **Numerical issues:** the trapezoidal companion model in SFA can produce sustained numerical oscillations; the literature recommends L-stable integrators (SDIRK/TR-BDF2) — see the "two-stage/three-stage SDIRK for SFEMT" papers. Frequency-matched integrators (Demiray) improve accuracy near the shift frequency.
- **Engineering effort vs. simpler wins.** A full complex hybrid solver is a substantial rewrite. The §5 optimizations recover much of the CPU budget at far lower risk and should be proven first; the phasor rewrite should be justified against what remains after those are in place.

### 5. Simpler / complementary optimizations to do first

1. **LU factorization reuse.** When topology and timestep are fixed, the MNA conductance matrix is constant; only the RHS (history sources, source values) changes per tick. Factor once (LU), then do only forward/back-substitution each tick — turning an O(n³) factorization into an O(n²) (dense) or near-O(nnz) (sparse) solve. Refactor only on topology/switch/timestep changes. This alone can dominate the savings for static networks. (Confirm whether the current Apache-Commons-Math-based path already caches this; if it rebuilds the matrix object each step it is silently refactoring.)
2. **Subsystem partitioning (already in ELN).** Keep the `RootSystem`/`SubSystem` split so only subsystems whose inputs changed re-solve. A subsystem with a steady load and steady source need not solve at all between ticks.
3. **Observer-gated fidelity.** Only subsystems with an oscilloscope (or meter needing waveform detail) attached run at high fidelity / synthesize waveforms; all others run in cheap phasor or even static mode. This is the single highest-leverage, lowest-risk optimization for this specific mod, because scopes are rare relative to total circuitry.
4. **Multirate solving.** Substep only nonlinear/fast devices (diodes, switching stages) at a fine rate while the linear network is solved coarsely — LIM/partitioned-LIM and classic multirate EMT. Complements both transient and phasor modes.
5. **SIMD / vectorization.** Complex or 2N-real stamps and back-substitution vectorize well; batch subsystem solves. In C# use `System.Numerics.Vector<T>` / hardware intrinsics and contiguous struct-of-arrays storage.
6. **Partial refactorization** (factorization-path / KLU-style) when only a few entries change (one switch), instead of a full refactor.

### 6. Implementation considerations for Vintage Story (C#)

- **Reusing the MNA structure.** The complex admittance MNA has the *same sparsity and stamping structure* as the real conductance MNA — only the scalar type changes. Two implementation routes: (i) a `Complex` element type (C# `System.Numerics.Complex`), or (ii) the **equivalent real formulation**: replace each complex entry `a+jb` with a 2×2 real block `[[a, −b],[b, a]]`, doubling matrix dimension but reusing the existing real LU path and vectorizing cleanly. Route (ii) is usually the least disruptive to an existing real MNA codebase and avoids a complex linear-algebra dependency.
- **Vintage Story runtime facts.** VS mods are C#; the server runs at a **default 30 TPS**. The Vintage Story Wiki server-config documentation lists `"TickTime": 33.333332` and states: *"Desired amount of milliseconds between each server tick. Default value is 30 ticks per second, i.e. 1000/30 = 33.333ms."* MSPT should stay under 33 ms,\[5\] so the electrical solver's per-tick budget is a few ms at most when sharing the tick with everything else. The recent .NET runtime (VS 1.22 community perf mods report .NET 10) gives good JIT/SIMD and Server GC; **minimize per-tick heap allocations** (reuse matrix/vector buffers; the VS perf-modding community specifically calls out per-tick allocation as a stutter source). Run the electrical sim off the main thread if the mod framework allows, and persist phasor state (complex `X_k` per node/branch) in NBT for save/load.
- **Component → phasor-domain model map:**
  - *Resistor:* `G` (real) in both modes.
  - *Inductor / Capacitor:* SFA complex companion admittance (frequency-dependent) in phasor mode; Dommel Norton companion in transient mode.
  - *AC voltage source / generator:* complex phasor injection; magnitude = wind-driven amplitude (updated per tick); frequency = tracked carrier. In transient mode, the instantaneous sinusoid.
  - *Transformer:* complex turns-ratio coupling (ELN already models transformers via an `InterSystem` process — reuse that coupling, complexified).
  - *Rectifier:* switching-function coupling between k=0 (DC) and k=1,…(AC) phasor blocks, or keep in transient mode (recommended initially).
  - *Motor / machine:* dynamic-phasor machine model (Stankovic/Zhang) with chosen harmonic set; or transient mode.
  - *Resistive/steady loads:* trivial in both modes.
- **State migration between modes:** transient→phasor via one-cycle sliding-window DFT of node waveforms; phasor→transient via `Re{Σ_k X_k·e^{jkωt₀}}` reconstruction of instantaneous states (including inductor currents/capacitor voltages) at the splice instant.
- **Testing strategy.** Build a golden-reference harness: run the legacy 1 kHz real-valued MNA and the new phasor solver on identical scenarios (steady wind, ramping wind, switch closure, rectifier turn-on, two-generator beat) and compare (a) RMS/peak error of the reconstructed waveform vs. the transient reference, (b) visual scope overlay, (c) continuity at splice points. Accept phasor mode only where waveform error stays within a set tolerance; otherwise fall back to transient. DPsim (which runs both domains on the same model) is a useful external cross-check for individual component models.

## Recommendations

**Phase 0 — Baseline & instrumentation (prerequisite).** Add MSPT/solve-time counters per subsystem; capture reference waveforms from the current 1 kHz solver for a fixed scenario suite. *Threshold to proceed:* confirm the electrical solve is actually a measurable fraction of tick time (if it is not, stop — the CPU problem is elsewhere).

**Phase 1 — Cheap wins (low risk, do regardless).** Implement LU factor-and-reuse (RHS-only back-substitution when the matrix is unchanged), verify/repair subsystem partitioning so unchanged subsystems skip solving, and add observer-gated fidelity (only scope-attached subsystems run at full rate). Add SIMD/struct-of-arrays storage. *Benchmark:* target a large reduction in per-tick solve time on static networks; re-measure before committing to Phase 2.

**Phase 2 — Dynamic-phasor quasi-steady mode + scope synthesis.** Implement the complex (or 2N-real) MNA, single dominant harmonic (k=1), fixed nominal carrier. Wind amplitude → per-tick source-phasor update. Scope synthesizes `Re{X·e^{jωt}}`. Validate against the golden reference for steady and slowly-ramping wind. *Threshold to proceed to Phase 3:* waveform RMS error within tolerance in steady/quasi-steady scenarios and net CPU win confirmed.

**Phase 3 — Transient splice + handoff.** Add event detection, phasor→transient initialization, adaptive 1 kHz transient burst, steady-state detection, and transient→phasor DFT extraction (FAST / SFA–EMT pattern). Validate continuity (no visible discontinuity) on switch-closure and fault scenarios. Add carrier-frequency tracking for variable-speed generators. *Threshold:* clean splices and correct two-generator beat behavior.

**Phase 4 — Multi-harmonic extensions.** Add harmonic sets for rectifiers (k=0/2/6) and machines; measure the harmonic-count vs. cost crossover per subsystem and auto-select phasor-vs-transient accordingly. Use L-stable integrators (SDIRK/TR-BDF2) if numerical oscillation appears.

**What would change these recommendations:** If Phase 0 shows the solver is not CPU-dominant, skip Phases 2–4 entirely. If Phase 1 already brings MSPT comfortably under budget, treat Phases 2–4 as a *quality* improvement (smoother scopes) rather than a performance necessity, and scope effort accordingly. If most in-game circuits contain rectifiers/motors needing many harmonics, the phasor advantage shrinks and a well-optimized transient solver (Phase 1 + multirate + LIM) may be the better long-term bet.

## Caveats

- **ELN internals are partly inferred.** The public ELN wiki, libage README, and config keys confirm the `RootSystem`/`SubSystem`/`Component→Bipole` architecture, real-valued/time-domain solve, Apache Commons Math dependency, and the oversampling config keys. The exact electrical timestep `dt`, the precise L/C companion form (backward-Euler vs. trapezoidal), whether the LU factorization is cached, and the strict absence of Newton–Raphson could **not** be verified from source (GitHub raw/tree access was blocked during research). Confirm these by reading `mods/eln/sim/mna/{RootSystem,SubSystem}.java`, `misc/MnaConst.java`, `component/{Capacitor,Inductor}.java`, and the libage Kotlin `org.libage.sim.electrical.mna` sources directly before implementation.
- **Speedup figures are scenario-dependent.** The ">50×" (up to ">70×", Zhang/UBC) and "2.33–5.5×" (frequency-shifted rational approximation) numbers come from power-system test cases, not this mod. The reliable, mod-specific figure is the ~30× reduction in solves/second from moving the network solve from 1 kHz to 30 Hz, partially offset by complex-arithmetic and multi-harmonic costs. Treat any single global "speedup" number as an estimate pending Phase-2 benchmarks.
- **Validity boundary is a hard constraint, not a tunable.** Dynamic phasors are only correct when envelope bandwidth ≪ carrier. Beating between asynchronous generators, fast switching, and faults can violate this; the hybrid's transient mode exists precisely to cover those cases, and the steady-state detector must be conservative to avoid handing back to phasor mode too early.
- **Some cited sources are secondary.** Reference bibliographic details (volumes/pages/years) were cross-checked across multiple citing works and publisher pages; verify DOIs against IEEE Xplore before formal publication. DPsim (github.com/sogno-platform/dpsim) and the Demiray thesis are open-access and should be consulted directly as primary implementation references.
